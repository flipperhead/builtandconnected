The Technology Checkup

Most technology trouble isn't dramatic. It's a backup nobody ever tested, a router still using the password it shipped with, an email account with no second layer of protection. Small things, quietly unattended, until the day they matter.

The Checkup is a couple of hours at your place going through all of it, and a written report you keep — what I found, what to fix first, and what can wait. Most people book it once, fix the handful of things that matter, and don't need me again for a while. That's the idea.

What I look at

  • Your devices — what's on the network, and whether anything on it shouldn't be
  • Backups — whether they exist, and whether a file actually comes back
  • Accounts — email, banking, and cloud sign-ins, and how you'd recover them
  • Router and Wi-Fi — admin password, firmware, guest network, coverage
  • Updates — what's out of date, and what's no longer supported at all
  • Protection — what's running, whether it's working, and whether you're paying twice

When something's wrong

The call most people make first

The computer is slow, stuck, or acting strange

Windows or Mac. Sorting out what's actually happening before deciding whether it's worth fixing or worth replacing.

Wi-Fi that drops, crawls, or won't reach

Dead spots in the back bedroom, a signal that dies on the deck, a connection that drops every evening at the same time.

Email trouble

Messages that won't send, a mailbox that stopped syncing, or an account you've been locked out of.

Printers

Yes, really. It's one of the most common calls and one of the most annoying problems to have.

Something looks like a scam

A message, a pop-up, a phone call, a text about a package you never ordered. Send it to me before you click it.

Files you think are gone

Photos, documents, a drive that stopped showing up. Sometimes recoverable, sometimes not — I'll tell you honestly which.

Setting things up

Done once, done properly

New computer setup and moving your files

Everything transferred, the new machine configured the way you actually work, and the old one wiped before it leaves the house.

Network and Wi-Fi

Router placement, coverage where you need it, a separate network for guests, and the admin password changed off the factory default.

Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace

Email, calendars, and file storage set up so they work across your devices without three copies of everything.

Cable runs, mounts, and enclosures

Ethernet through the wall, a camera or access point mounted properly, a cabinet built for the equipment. The carpentry side earns its keep here.

Keeping it safe

The part most people skip

Backups, with a tested restore

Plenty of people have a backup. Far fewer have watched a file come back out of it. I set it up, then prove it works and show you the proof.

Accounts and sign-in

Second-factor sign-in on the accounts that matter, recovery details brought up to date, and a password manager you'll actually use.

Learning to spot a scam

An hour or so, one-on-one or with a few neighbours, going through the real ones — and a single page you can keep by the phone.

Router and Wi-Fi hardening

Firmware updated, default credentials replaced, remote administration switched off, guest traffic kept away from your own devices.

For small businesses

Too small for an IT company, too busy to do it yourself

A four-person office, a lodge, a shop, a restaurant. Enough technology that an outage costs you a day, but not enough to justify a monthly contract with a firm down in Loveland.

The work is the same as above, scaled up: the network, the accounts, the backups, the machines. The difference is what happens when it breaks, because for you it isn't an inconvenience, it's the register not opening.

  • Call when you need me — no contract, no minimum, no monthly fee
  • Come back periodically — a lot of businesses do this once or twice a year

What I don't do

I'm not a managed service provider. I don't sell monthly contracts, I don't watch your systems overnight, and I won't tell you there's a help desk when the help desk is one person who was asleep. If you need someone monitoring a network around the clock, you need a company with staff, and I'll tell you that rather than take the work.

I also don't do alarm systems, satellite dishes, or television repair, and I'm not an electrician. If a job turns out to need one, I'll say so before you're out any money.

What I do is come to your place, fix what's in front of me, set things up so they hold, and explain it clearly enough that you're not dependent on me next time.

Tell me what's going on

Most jobs start with a short phone call about what's happening and whether I'm the right person for it. Sometimes it's a ten-minute fix over the phone, and that call is free either way.
