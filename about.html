About

Twenty-five years
in Estes Park

I'm Jason Seaver. I run Built & Connected on my own — the carpentry and the computer work both.

In May of 2001 I was driving from the East Coast to the West Coast and stopped in Estes Park to see friends. The visit turned into a summer, and I never finished the drive.

Twenty-five years later I'm still here, and most of that time I have spent building. Large new construction on one end, small craftsman work on the other, and most of what falls between. Decks, remodels, finish work, and the long list of repairs that come with houses at eight thousand feet.

I came up through the trade on job sites, working for other people and eventually running the work. As a lead carpenter the quality of the finished job was my responsibility. That is where I learned the part that matters in any trade: knowing when something is right, and not leaving it when it isn't.

Why I moved into computer work

Computers and networks have interested me my whole life, and for a long time that stayed a hobby.

What turned it into work was seeing how much damage ordinary scams do. A retired couple loses twenty years of photographs because no one ever set up a backup. That one is common, and an afternoon of work prevents it.

Nearly all of it is preventable in an afternoon.

What I help people keep is straightforward: their accounts, their photographs, and their business running. Most of it comes down to a few things set up properly and checked once in a while.

So I did the training rather than leaving it a hobby. I completed a cybersecurity program through the University of Colorado and hold a CompTIA Security+ certification. I keep a lab at home, where I spend most evenings taking things apart and working out how to put them back.

When a job is outside what I do, I say so and point you to someone who handles it.

Doing both trades

I'm a tradesman. Some days that means a deck and some days it means a network. Both come down to understanding how something is put together, finding the part that is failing, and fixing it so it stays fixed.

The combination is useful more often than you would expect. Computer work in a house is physical. Cable has to run through a wall. A second Wi-Fi unit needs mounting where the signal is needed rather than where it is convenient. A camera goes on trim that splits if you don't know what you are doing. The equipment needs somewhere to live that isn't the floor of a closet.

I do both halves of that work, so it's one visit and no handoff in the middle.

You will have a price before I start. If I find something I didn't expect, you hear about it that day rather than at the end.

Outside work

I have been rock climbing for thirty years. It is most of the reason I stopped here in the first place and most of the reason I stayed.

My daughter was born and raised in Estes Park and has just started college. There is also Ayla, nine years old, who has spent the last few weeks wandering the house wondering where everyone went.

If we end up working together you will probably hear about all of it. That is how it goes when you hire someone local.
