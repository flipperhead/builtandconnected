Two kinds of problem

A rental property fails in two directions. The guest can't get onto the Wi-Fi, the lock code doesn't work, or the thermostat has been set to eighty since Tuesday.

Or the deck rail is loose, the screen door won't latch, and something is rotting where the snow sits.

Both cost you the same thing: a bad review, a refund, and a scramble to find somebody who can come today. Normally they are two calls to two people. I handle both.

The technology side

Set up so it survives turnover

Guest Wi-Fi on its own network

Guests get their own network. Your equipment and any owner devices sit on a separate one, so guests can't reach them. The guest password is easy to type on a phone and easy to change between stays.

Coverage that reaches the whole place

Stone walls, log construction, and a router in the wrong corner all cut the signal short. I add a second Wi-Fi unit where the signal is needed, with the cable run and mounted cleanly.

Door locks and codes

Locks set up under your account, with codes that change between guests. You get a written procedure, so a cleaner leaving doesn't mean losing control of the door.

Thermostats and remote checks

Set up so you can see and change the temperature from wherever you are, with sensible limits. A guest can't leave it running at eighty with the windows open.

Televisions and streaming

Signed out of your personal accounts, set up so guests can use their own, and reset between stays.

A printed sheet for the house

Network name, password, how the TV works, what the thermostat does. One page, laminated, on the counter. It cuts down the number of texts you get.

The building side

Between guests, before it becomes urgent

Decks, rails, and stairs

The parts guests lean on and step off. Rot repair, loose rail posts, treads that have started to move, and rebuilds when it is past patching.

Doors, screens, and hardware

The door that swelled shut, the screen a dog went through, the latch that only works a certain way. Everything in a rental has to work for somebody who has never been in the house before.

Wear and turnover damage

Trim knocked loose, a cabinet door off its hinge, a shelf pulled out of the wall. Small things that make a place look neglected in photographs.

Weather damage

Snow load, ice, and sun are hard on anything outdoors up here. I know what fails first and roughly when.

Worth checking

Who owns the accounts

This one is easy to miss. A cleaner, a former manager, or a handy relative sets up the lock or the thermostat on their own phone, under their own account. It works fine for two years.

Then they stop working for you, and the account, the codes, and the ability to let anyone in go with them.

The same thing happens with the router, the camera, and the streaming logins. It is rarely anyone's fault. It is what happens when nobody was thinking about it at setup.

What I check

  • Ownership — every device and account in your name, with others added as users you can remove
  • Codes — who has one, whether it changes, and whether you would know if it didn't
  • Guests kept separate — they can't reach your equipment, cameras, or files
  • Cameras — placed and disclosed properly, exterior only, in line with platform rules
  • Recovery — what happens if you lose the phone the whole thing is tied to
  • Written down — a document with the accounts, devices, and passwords, kept somewhere you will find it

Twice a year

The seasonal visit

Finding out from a guest is the worst time to find out something is broken, and the most expensive. A visit before the season starts catches a good share of it first.

I walk the property and go through both sides. I test the Wi-Fi in every room, check lock batteries and rotate codes, and confirm the thermostat still reports. Then I look over the deck, rails, stairs, doors, and anywhere water has been sitting.

You get a short written list of what needs doing, what can wait, and what I would fix while I am standing there.

Quoted per property after a look at the place. No contract and no monthly fee — most owners book one before summer and one before the snow.

If you use a property manager

  • I work alongside them — and I'll talk to them directly if that is easier
  • You keep the accounts — managers change; the door shouldn't have to
  • The report goes to you — and to them as well, if you would rather
  • Several properties? — tell me how many and we'll work out something that makes sense

What I don't offer

  • Whole-house automation with scenes and voice control
  • Emergency or guaranteed same-day response
  • Alarm systems
  • Electrical and plumbing

If you need one of these, I'll point you to someone who does.
