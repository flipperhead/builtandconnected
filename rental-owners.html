Two kinds of problem, two different phone numbers

A rental property fails in two directions. The guest can't get onto the Wi-Fi, or the lock code doesn't work, or the thermostat has been set to eighty since Tuesday. Or the deck rail is loose, the screen door won't latch, and something is rotting where the snow sits.

Both cost you the same thing — a bad review, a refund, a scramble to find somebody who can come today. And normally they're two separate calls to two people who each say the other part isn't their department.

I do both. That's the entire pitch.

The technology side

Set up so it survives turnover

Guest Wi-Fi, separated properly

Guests on their own network, your equipment and any owner devices on another. A password that's easy to type on a phone and easy to change between stays.

Coverage that reaches the whole place

Stone walls, log construction, and a router in the wrong corner. Access points placed where the signal actually needs to be, cable run and mounted cleanly.

Smart locks and codes

Locks set up under your account, codes that change between guests, and a written procedure so a cleaner leaving doesn't mean losing control of the door.

Thermostats and remote checks

Set up so you can see and change it from wherever you are, with sensible limits — and so a guest can't leave it running at eighty with the windows open.

Televisions and streaming

Signed out of your personal accounts, set up so guests can use their own, and reset between stays.

A printed sheet for the house

Network name, password, how the TV works, what the thermostat does. One page, laminated, on the counter. It cuts the number of texts you get.

The building side

Between guests, before it becomes urgent

Decks, rails, and stairs

The parts guests lean on and step off. Rot repair, loose rail posts, treads that have started to move, and rebuilds when it's past patching.

Doors, screens, and hardware

The door that swelled shut, the screen a dog went through, the latch that only works if you know the trick. Guests don't know the trick.

Wear and turnover damage

Trim knocked loose, a cabinet door off its hinge, a shelf pulled out of the wall. Small things that make a place feel neglected in photographs.

Weather damage

Snow load, ice, and sun are hard on anything outdoors up here. I know what fails first and roughly when.

Worth checking today

Whose name is the lock in?

This is the one that catches people out. A cleaner, a former manager, or a handy relative sets up the lock or the thermostat on their own phone, under their own account. It works fine for two years.

Then they stop working for you — and the account, the codes, and the ability to let anyone in go with them. Sometimes on bad terms.

The same thing happens with the router, the camera, and the streaming logins. It's rarely anyone's fault. It's just what happens when nobody was thinking about it at setup.

What I check

  • Ownership — every device and account in your name, with others added as users you can remove
  • Codes — who has one, whether it changes, and whether you'd know if it didn't
  • Network separation — guests can't reach your equipment, cameras, or files
  • Cameras — placed and disclosed properly, exterior only, in line with platform rules
  • Recovery — what happens if you lose the phone the whole thing is tied to
  • Written down — a document with the accounts, devices, and passwords, kept somewhere you'll find it

Twice a year

The seasonal visit

Most owners find out something is broken when a guest tells them, which is the worst time and the most expensive. A visit before the season starts catches a good share of it first.

I walk the property and go through both sides: test the Wi-Fi in every room, check lock batteries and rotate codes, confirm the thermostat still reports, and look over the deck, rails, stairs, doors, and anywhere water has been sitting.

You get a short written list of what needs doing, what can wait, and what I'd fix now while I'm standing there.

Quoted per property after a look at the place. No contract, no monthly fee — most owners book one before summer and one before the snow.

If you use a property manager

  • I work alongside them — not instead of them, and I'll talk to them directly if that's easier
  • You keep the accounts — managers change; the door shouldn't have to
  • The report goes to you — and to them as well, if you'd rather
  • Managing several properties? — tell me how many and we'll work out something that makes sense

Where the line is

I set up smart locks, thermostats, and cameras, and I'm good at the part that usually goes wrong — accounts, access, codes, and making sure the network underneath is sound. If you want an integrated whole-house automation system with scenes and voice control, I'm not your person, and I'll say so rather than learn on your money.

I'm also not an emergency service. If a guest is locked out at eleven at night, I may well answer, but I'm one person and I won't promise you a response time I can't keep every single time. Anyone who does is guessing.

Tell me about the property

Where it is, how many bedrooms, what's been giving you trouble. I'll tell you what I'd look at and what it would cost — and if part of it isn't something I do, I'll point you at who does.
